LIVING GDD UPDATE
What changed from the midterm GDD — and why.
⚠ Project Pivot Notice
The midterm GDD described Hollow Sprint, a solo 2D precision-platformer built in Unity. During early production, it became clear that the scope — custom scar/death system, dynamic lighting pipeline, level streaming — was too large for the available time and team. Rather than ship a broken version of Hollow Sprint, we pivoted to the Excavator Co-op Party Game, a concept that was already prototyped and demonstrated stronger playtest engagement. This document reflects the final state of that shipped build.

Core Concept — What Actually Shipped

Game Overview

A local co-op party game for two players who share a single excavator with an unusual dual-arm design. Players must work together (or hilariously fail together) to navigate a collapsing construction site and get home safe. The game takes place in a near-future world where urban construction has spiraled out of control, leaving workers stranded on unstable scaffold towers with no way down except through.

Built in Unreal Engine. Published on itch.io as a free vertical-slice demo with Steam integration scaffolded for a future commercial release.

Player 1 — Driver
Controls the left arm and handles acceleration. Responsible for forward motion and balance. Gets blamed first when everything goes wrong.
Player 2 — Steerer
Controls the right arm and handles steering. Responsible for direction and grappling. Equally at fault when everything goes wrong.

What Was Cut — Scope Reductions

Mechanics Removed During Production
Feature Original Plan Why It Was Cut Status
4-Player 2v2 Mode Two excavators, optional sabotage Local 4-player input routing in Unreal took longer than estimated; split-screen camera rig was unstable Cut
Swinging Crane Loads Physics grapple targets mid-level Physics constraint jitter was never resolved within the sprint; would have broken pacing Cut
Collapsing Concrete Blocks Timed escape sequences Required networked destruction events; out of scope for a local co-op build Cut
Steam Achievements Fully implemented achievement list Steam SDK integrated but achievement triggers not wired; deferred to post-launch patch Cut

What Evolved Through Playtesting

Mechanics That Changed Shape
Feature Original Design What Actually Happened Status
Physics Controls Realistic, simulation-grade arm physics Playtesters found pure simulation frustrating. We added "sticky grab" assist and arm-angle snapping to make failure feel comedic rather than punishing. The sloppiness became a feature. Evolved
Narrow Scaffold Levels Purely about not falling off Playtesters invented their own meta-game of intentionally bumping each other. We added a "nudge" input and made scaffold edges slightly rounded to reward the chaos play style. Evolved
2-Player Co-op Cooperative only Fully shipped as designed. Kept the asymmetric role split (driver/steerer) — this was the single most-praised element in playtest sessions. Kept
Steam Integration Full SDK with matchmaking Only overlay and local co-op detection shipped. Online matchmaking was out of scope; presence data is logged but not used. Evolved
Camera System Fixed perspective Evolved into a dynamic spring-arm camera that follows the center-of-mass between both players, with shake on impact. This was not planned but became essential after playtests. Evolved

Final Core Loop (As Shipped)

~5 Minute Experience — Whitebox State

Main Menu → Functional menu with Host and Join buttons. Steam session is created or joined here.

Spawn → Both players load into a single whitebox level. Controls are discovered through play.

Traverse → Players navigate the level using the dual-arm physics system. The grab/pull mechanic is partially functional; behavior is inconsistent and remains a known issue.

End of Level → Players reach the end of the whitebox level in approximately 5 minutes. There is no formal win screen or Game Over state — the loop does not yet close cleanly.

Known Gap: The core loop is not fully closed. A Game Over / win state and restart flow were planned but not implemented in this build.

DEVELOPER POST-MORTEM
A critical reflection on the full development arc — both projects.
This post-mortem covers both the abandoned Hollow Sprint prototype and the shipped Excavator Co-op vertical slice. The failure of the first project is as instructive as the success of the second.

What Went Right

✓ Successes
The pivot decision itself. Recognizing that Hollow Sprint's scope was unmanageable early enough to ship something else required real honesty. Most studios sink more time into broken projects out of sunk-cost thinking. Cutting it at the right moment was the most important call made in this production.
Asymmetric controls as a design anchor. The driver/steerer split gave the game an immediately legible identity. Every playtester understood their role within 30 seconds. Simple asymmetry created complex emergent communication — players started narrating the machine's mood to each other, which was never planned.
Physics-based comedy. Rather than fight the jank, we leaned into it. The sticky grab assist made the excavator feel lovably incompetent rather than frustrating. This reframe — failure is fun, not punishment — was the most important design insight of the project.
Steam SDK integration. Getting the Steam overlay functional, even without full achievement wiring, was a technical win. It signals that the build is production-aware even if the feature set is incomplete.
Dynamic camera. The spring-arm center-of-mass camera emerged from a playtest crash fix and ended up being the most-praised technical element. It was never on any design doc. Happy accidents require a team that notices them.

What Went Wrong

✗ Failures & Mistakes
Hollow Sprint's scope was never honestly estimated. The midterm GDD read more like a dream board than a production plan. Dynamic lighting pipelines, procedural scar systems, and mobile-future targets were on the list for a solo developer in a single semester. The GDD had no cut scope, no fallback version, no risk log.
Engine choice mismatch on the first project. Unity's URP was chosen for Hollow Sprint primarily because it was "industry standard." It was also the engine the team knew least well under time pressure. Unreal — which the team had more reps in — would have been the smarter call from day one.
4-player mode was promised without a prototype. The GDD listed 4-player 2v2 as a core mode. It was never prototyped before being written into the design doc. This is a fundamental process error — no feature should be in a GDD that hasn't survived at least a paper test.
Physics constraint jitter was left unresolved too long. The swinging crane grapple mechanic failed in Unreal due to constraint instability. Two weeks passed before the decision was made to cut it. Earlier escalation would have saved time.
No UI/UX documentation until late in production. The main menu, pause screen, and HUD were built reactively rather than from a wireframe. The result is functional but not as readable as it should be. This would have been avoided with a Figma pass at week 2.

What We Would Do Differently With 6 More Months

→ If We Had More Time
Ship 4-player mode — the right way. The 2v2 sabotage mechanic was genuinely compelling in paper tests. With six months, we would prototype it in week 1, validate the fun, and build the input system from the ground up for 4 players rather than patching a 2-player architecture.
Build a proper level editor or modular level pipeline. The current scaffold layout is hand-placed. A modular procedural system — even a simple weighted tile-picker — would give infinite replayability and remove the biggest reason to stop playing after the first loop.
Add online co-op. The Steam SDK is already integrated. Online play would open the game to the largest possible audience and is the most commercially meaningful feature missing from the current build. Six months is enough time to ship a basic P2P connection.
Commission proper audio. Sound design is placeholder throughout. The physical comedy of the excavator deserves a full Foley pass — the clank of metal on scaffold, the strain of the engine, the comedic silence before a fall. Good audio would double the perceived quality of the build.
Run structured playtests at every milestone, not just the end. All the best changes in this project came from watching people play. We didn't start doing this until week 6. If playtesting had started at week 1, the nudge mechanic, the sticky grab, and the camera fix would have shipped in half the time.

🚧 EXCAVATOR CO-OP — Vertical Slice Demo

Two players. One machine. Zero coordination. Maximum chaos.

You and a friend share one excavator with two arms and two very different jobs. One of you drives. One of you steers. Neither of you has done this before.

Navigate a crumbling construction scaffold by grappling, swinging, and dragging your overweight machine upward — platform by platform — while trying to convince your co-pilot that whatever just happened was definitely their fault.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

HOW IT WORKS

Player 1 (Driver) — Controls the left arm + acceleration.
Player 2 (Steerer) — Controls the right arm + steering.

The arms aren't for digging. They're for grabbing onto steel scaffold beams, swinging across gaps, and hauling your enormous machine upward using nothing but physics and communication.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

WHAT'S IN THIS BUILD

• 1 whitebox level (~5 min playthrough)
• Physics-based dual-arm control system (partially functional)
• Main menu with Host / Join via Steam
• Steam session integration
• Gamepad required (2× controllers recommended)

⚠ NOT YET IN THIS BUILD: game over screen, checkpoints, final art, audio, HUD

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

ABOUT THIS RELEASE

This is a student vertical slice — a proof of concept, not a finished game. It was built in Unreal Engine as a final project submission. This is an early prototype — the grab mechanic is not fully resolved, and there is no game over screen or audio. What you are playing is the core physics concept in whitebox form. Online multiplayer, additional levels, and 4-player mode are planned if development continues.

If you try it, please leave a rating or comment. Every piece of feedback helps.
